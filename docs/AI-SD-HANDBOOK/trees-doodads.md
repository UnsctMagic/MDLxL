# Trees and doodads

A trunk can begin with 5–6 sides and two or three tapering rings; use a real fork only where the outline needs it. Around 16–48 triangles is a useful small-trunk target, while larger named native trunk territories are more variable. Bend the trunk slightly and broaden the base. Paint bark grooves and small knots instead of extruding them.

Foliage uses a few deliberate crossed or layered alpha cards, with meaningful gaps and the native leaf mask. Rocks need irregular large planes and a flattened ground side, not a dense sphere with noise. Mushrooms use a low-sided cap and stem, with gills and speckles painted. A thin flat leaf is valid when its material and reverse view support it.

Use checked bark/leaf/stone regions and preserve their alpha. Vary branching direction, canopy proportion, card angle, rock silhouette and base width; random rotation alone is not a new shape. Check the back and top, the ground contact and whether the foliage hides the trunk join. Do not add collision, sway bones or glow as an unsolicited improvement.

## Measured evidence and limits

Unit models only, excluding portrait files: trunk: 4 territories, median 6 triangles; horn: 11 territories, median 2 triangles. Heroes and differently named bone territories are still mixed; inspect the comparable role.

These counts come from [installed game measurements](../AI-SD-PART-DATA.json). A bone territory may be one segment or several joined parts; portraits, scenery and variants differ. Targets above are construction guidance, not measured percentiles or universal caps. Unknown classifications stay unknown. No source geometry is supplied.
