# Cloth, capes, banners and hair

A banner or cape is a deliberately thin sheet with 2–4 columns and a few rows where it bends or attaches, usually around 4–16 triangles for a simple hanging panel. Use the material's deliberate two-sided flag or matching reversed surface when required; do not accidentally leave the back invisible. Cloth may be thin: the rule for solid weapon forms does not require turning every cape into a box. Paint small folds, weave and seams.

A banner needs a sturdy 4–5 sided pole, a convincing top attachment and broad negative space between cloth and pole. A named faction's symbol belongs on its sheet, large enough to read. If no native sheet carries the required specific emblem, an original modest painted diffuse is justified; do not pretend a native colour swatch supplies it. Vary the bottom cut, main bend, width, attachment and pole top, not the amount of tiny decoration.

Hair uses a few solid tapering locks for silhouette and deliberate alpha cards for fine strands. Named native hair territories are small; do not model every strand. Paint connected highlight masses and shadowed roots. Orc topknots, human tufts, elven long hair and undead ragged cloth keep different outlines. Preserve existing alpha and team-colour regions on edits.

Give hanging cloth one dominant directional bend and perhaps one secondary fold; paint the finer creases. Use an asymmetrical hem or a different attachment span for variation. For hair, change the mass and lock grouping before adding strands. Preserve the empty spaces between the main locks so the silhouette stays readable.


A basic wizard hat can use a 5–6 sided brim and a crown of two or three tapering rings with one bent tip, about 50–90 triangles including the underside and head opening. Align the opening with the crown base and keep the brim attached. Use a genuine cloth region, such as the checked purple cape folds on units/undead/herodeathknight/herodeathknight.dds, with continuous UV direction across the rings. A plain request does not require a stitched seam or ornamental band. A purple armour panel with silver edging is unsuitable for a continuous cloth surface.
