# Orc axes and cleavers

An axe starts with a 4–5 sided wood haft and an extruded head outline. Give the head a broad cutting edge, a blunt poll and a thick central socket; sink the haft into that socket. Use roughly 8–32 triangles for the head and 8–24 for the shaft. A cleaver is a thick wedge blade with a heavy square or clipped tip, not a human sword decorated with spikes.

Make the head conspicuously wide and the handle sturdy: a head width around one third to one half of total height is a useful starting choice. Paint the steel bevel, uneven wood grain, leather ties and small iron fasteners. Use a checked Grunt or Hellscream steel/wood region if its palette fits. Keep the cutting edge on the highlight strip instead of placing half the blade over painted skin.

Choose one construction idea: a crude stone/bone edge, chipped black iron or a forged broad cleaver. Vary the asymmetry, head slope, edge notch and haft bend. A notch that changes the outline can use geometry; little chips and scratches are paint. Do not add many identical horns or rivet meshes. Check the back of the head, haft join and hand clearance from both sides.
