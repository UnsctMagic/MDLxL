# Skulls and bones

Study the native Skeleton's head and skull texture as references. Keep its geometry separate from a fresh result unless the user asks to reshape the native model. Its visible Head-bone territory has 96 triangles and 57 distinct posed positions at Stand; it is a shaped cranium, cheeks and mouth, not a cube. The complete native head/jaw union (Head and its five facial child bones) has 126 distinct triangles; the single Head-bone count does not include the complete mandible. Inspect both in the Library preview described in basics.md. A fresh ordinary skull can start around 80–130 triangles, with contour and coherent anatomy deciding whether it works. Do not squeeze its jaw into a slab to hit an arbitrary total.

## Shape one half, map it, then mirror

With front +X, symmetry is across Y=0. Design one half of the cranium with a rounded but faceted braincase, sloping forehead, cut-back temple, cheek projection and a smaller projecting upper jaw. Use unequal low-resolution cross sections or reshape a coarse general ellipsoid. Narrow the rear/crown and pinch below the cheek; do not leave the front as one rectangular wall. The lower jaw is a compact U-shaped mandible: a shallow curved chin/tooth rail, a thin horizontal side rail, a rear angle and a short rising arm. In SIDE view it has a concave L-shaped outline and an open notch above the rail. In TOP view it is a U, wrapping from the centre chin around each side. Put an actual corner at the rear angle before rising to the hinge. A single broad quad from front chin to rear hinge fills that notch and creates the rejected slab/beard shape. A few general extrusions along that path provide the required contour; the jaw does not need to be a thick closed box. Keep the mouth closed or slightly open, around 4–8% of total head height, rather than a tall square hole. Paint tooth divisions; do not build a row of little tooth boxes.

Preview textures/skeleton.dds. The checked skull-side-layout [0.5,0,1,0.5] is one continuous SIDE of a head. It contains one eye socket, half of the nose and the upper/lower tooth rows. Map your entire shaped half coherently in the XZ side view: rear cranium toward the left of this rectangle, nose toward its right edge, crown at the top, chin at the bottom. Adjust UV landmarks to the anatomy: socket near U .88/V .20, half nose near U 1/V .28, upper teeth around V .35, lower teeth around V .43. Give the lower tooth row only the short front lip faces. The long side rail and rear ascending arm use the corresponding bone/hinge part of the continuous layout, with a UV corner at the rear angle; do not stretch a diagonal front-to-hinge quad across the teeth. The small feature rectangles are landmarks/local repair regions, not a recipe for separate front-facing stickers. Keep cheek/cranium surfaces continuous between these landmarks. A generic planar fit is a starting layout, not proof that the eye, nose and mouth align.

Check the outer facial/cranium UV chart before mirroring with sd-native-uv inspectUVChart(geoset, selectedOuterHalfTriangleIds). Each anatomical landmark must lie inside its surrounding UV polygon. For example, the socket at U .88 cannot be an interior point of a panel whose UV boundary is entirely beyond U .94. That folds the painted face over itself. The connected outer half chart should have consistent nonzero UV winding; inspect reported possibleFoldFaces. Expand/rebuild the surrounding chart, not just the eye point. Treat bone caps, underside and mouth interiors as separate charts; opposite-winding separate mirrored islands are intentional. Inspect mesh facing with the existing NormalsXL APIs if needed, without changing an authored source globally.

Once the half reads correctly, inspect await api.describe('ai-mesh') and call mirrorSelection(model,{[geosetId]:allHalfVertexIds},{axis:'y',plane:0,weld:false}). This duplicates the half, reflects positions and normals, corrects triangle winding, and copies the SAME UVs unchanged. Do not flip U again: the stacked side islands produce the paired painted features. The ordinary Mirror command reflects in place; mirrorSelection duplicates first. Centre-plane positions must meet exactly. Weld only compatible centre vertices if necessary; keep intentional normal/UV splits. Flat-normal halves may meet without welded indices, but their positions must not leave a crack.

Check front, side, rear and underside. Eyes should sit on the sloping front/side anatomy, the nose must meet the midline, teeth stay at the mouth, and the mandible must rise to the rear cheek. Reject a flat face, a block jaw or a skull that only works from one camera. If the subject cannot fit this native side layout, justify an original modest diffuse; do not stretch the whole atlas over a frontal quad.

Choose independent variations for repeated skull requests: change cranium height/depth, brow slope, cheek projection and jaw width/projection. Change at least three relationships, not just colour or whole-model scale. Preserve continuous anatomy and the mirrored texture arrangement. A horn or tusk can use 3–5 sides and two or three tapering rings, around 6–24 triangles; shallow cracks and cavities belong in paint.

## Measured evidence and limits

Head: 890 named-bone territories; median 66 triangles, P10–P90 5–158. Unit-only heads: 373 territories, median 40 triangles. Bone names are proxies, and some territories include helmet, neck or adjoining surfaces; these are not universal caps. Horn: 59 territories, median 20 triangles, P10–P90 2–30. The checked native Skull example and method are in [installed game measurements](../AI-SD-PART-DATA.json). No source geometry is supplied.
