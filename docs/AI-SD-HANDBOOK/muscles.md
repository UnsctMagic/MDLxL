# Muscles

Use a few broad planes for the chest, deltoid and forearm. A single wider ring at a bicep and a taper toward the elbow is usually enough. A boxy chest can have one central ridge and an angled lower edge; it does not need separately modelled pectoral, abdominal and rib objects. Native named arm territories have small triangle counts because much muscle definition is painted.

Paint broad top-lit volumes: warm or lighter upper planes, darker contact under the chest and armpit, a restrained hue shift in shadows. Paint abdominal divisions and tendons as connected forms, not black outlines around every muscle. Keep the light direction consistent across mirrored UV islands.

Orcs use thick forearms and a compact neck; humans a clearer chest/waist taper; lean undead can use a few visible ribs with the rest painted. Change mass distribution and posture before adding edges. For an edit keep the source's pose and lower body outside scope. Check bent joints and avoid welding across a normal or weight boundary merely to remove duplicate positions.
