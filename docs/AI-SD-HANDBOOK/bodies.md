# Bodies and proportions

Build pelvis and chest as separate tapered boxes, joined by a small waist ring; use two boxy segments per limb. A new ordinary knight can start around 400–650 visible triangles, including equipment, with only enough rings for elbows, knees and the waist. Treat that as a design target, not a whole-file cap. Count visible Stand surfaces separately from corpse and effect geometry.

Use roughly 4.5–6 heads of body height, shoulders about 2–2.5 head widths and a wide stance as starting proportions. Hands, feet and weapon shapes read larger than realistic anatomy. Keep upper arms and thighs broad; the waist, wrists and ankles narrow enough to make negative spaces. Four or five perimeter points per limb ring usually suffice. Retain a clear elbow/knee bend in three-quarter view. Do not sculpt every tendon.

A human soldier uses an upright chest, blocky boots and layered plate silhouettes. An orc uses a wider chest, forward head and thick forearms; an elf uses a narrower torso and long tapering lines; an undead body can expose ribs through paint and a few purposeful gaps. Vary chest taper, stance, head size and equipment balance instead of repeating the same body with a new colour. For an existing body preserve its authored proportions, bones and weights. Check shoulder, elbow, knee and hip poses before calling a joint complete.
