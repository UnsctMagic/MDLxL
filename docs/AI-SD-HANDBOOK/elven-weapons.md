# Elven glaives and moonblades

Draw a new crescent or leaf outline with few deliberate corners. Extrude it into a shallow ridged solid with a thicker inner spine and tapered tips. One main crescent can begin around 16–40 triangles; a small grip around 8–20. Put corners at changes in curvature instead of sampling a perfect arc with dozens of segments. A glaive should read as a broad elegant crescent from the game camera, not a thin silver wire.

Human/high-elven blades tend to long leaf tapers, silver and restrained gold; night-elven blades use moon/crescent shapes, purple/teal and dark metal. Choose the identity the prompt names. Native Huntress or Demon Hunter sheets may provide metal and purple leather regions; preview them first and align the painted edge with the silhouette. Paint inlaid lines and shallow ornament.

Change whether the crescent opens forward or backward, its depth, tip lengths, spine thickness and grip placement. Keep the mass balanced around its held point. Do not produce identical mirrored discs every time, use a torus as the whole blade, or add a generic jewel just to fill the centre. Check edge thickness, both faces and the clear opening between blade and hand.
