# Creatures and wings

Block out the animal's torso and major limb masses first. Use low-sided tapering rings for legs, neck, horns and tails, putting rings at real changes of direction. A tail segment often needs only a few triangles. Vary chest/back slope, limb length and head mass by species; do not force all creatures onto a human body.

A wing is a broad readable membrane or feather card with a few bone/finger supports. Around 6–24 triangles for a simple wing surface is a starting target; the study's wing territories may divide a whole wing across several bones. Paint membrane folds and small feather divisions. Alpha cutouts are intentional; maintain their material mode and examine the reverse side.

Use native creature sheets for their intended islands, not a random rectangle over a whole animal. Match the palette and painted lighting between mirrored sides. For edits test an open and folded wing, tail extremes and ground contact. Disconnected components can be intentional claws or cards; inspect contact and animation before declaring them floating or welding them together.
