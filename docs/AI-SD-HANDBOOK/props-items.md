# Props and items

Decide whether the object is a dropped item, a hand-held prop or scenery. A basic ground item often needs only 60–140 triangles; a silhouette-heavy object may need more for a clear reason. A chalice can start with a 5–6 sided hollow bowl, a 4–5 sided stem and a low-sided foot. Use separate inner bowl faces so the cup is hollow, and connect or overlap the stem and foot without daylight. A narrow stem must stay chunky at game scale.

For a chalice worthy of Mordor, choose a dark iron body and a restrained fiery accent with a jagged architectural silhouette. One clear eye or angular crown motif may fit the named subject; do not put that motif on every evil cup. Keep spikes few and visible. Paint interior shadow, edge light and small engraved marks. Native dark metal may fit the body; if the exact emblem needs a new diffuse, paint it originally and keep it modest.

Crates/barrels use a few large faces with painted planks, bands and nails. Bottles use a low-sided body and distinct neck; gemstones need a few crisp facets. Vary proportions, main contour and one focal detail. Reject over-round bowls, dense torus rims, unrelated materials, repeated procedural stripes and undersides that reveal floating parts.
