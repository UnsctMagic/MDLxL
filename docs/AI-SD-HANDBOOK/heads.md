# Heads and faces

Start with a low ring around the brow, a wider cheek/jaw ring and a narrow chin; use a few front vertices to project the nose and brow. Around 40–90 triangles is a useful ordinary unit-head target; the named-head measurement median is not a mandate to simplify portraits. Avoid a smooth UV sphere with painted stickers. Eyes, nostrils, mouth creases, teeth divisions and eyebrow shapes usually belong in the texture. Give the nose, jaw, tusks or ears real outline when the view needs them.

A human face reads through a broad brow and jaw, an orc through jaw/tusks and a heavy brow, an elf through an angular cheek and long ear silhouette, an undead through the cranium/jaw contrast. Change jaw width, cranium slope, nose projection, cheek planes and ear placement independently. Keep eyes aligned with the painted sheet; do not map the whole head to one tiny skin swatch. For a face use the native head island with its real anatomical UV arrangement or paint an original diffuse if no sheet fits. A rectangular metal or skin region is a material patch, not a complete face unwrap.

Use 5–6 sides for a simple helmet shell, a brow lip and perhaps a nose guard. Paint engraving and seams. A plume is a tapered solid tuft or deliberate alpha cards. Check eye positions, jaw thickness and the head/neck join from behind as well as the front.
