# Shields

Draw a few-cornered shield outline and give it a shallow ridge or convex back, around 8–32 triangles for an ordinary unit shield. A round shield starts with 6–8 edge points, not 32. Use a broad painted rim instead of a separate bevel ring on every edge. A boss or emblem needs real volume only if it changes the intended outline or depth; paint flat heraldry.

A shield can be around 35–55% of body height as a starting design choice. Human shields favour a kite or squared heater, orcs broad wood/iron shapes, elves a long leaf taper, undead bone and broken iron. Look for checked Footman, Captain or race-appropriate sheets. A shield image must use its actual shield island; a generic rectangular crop cannot reconstruct a complex heraldic unwrap automatically.

Vary top width, lower point, centre ridge, rim thickness and emblem placement. Keep its back and grip structurally plausible. Avoid paper-thin shields viewed edge-on, floating symbols, dense concentric circles and reusing a source shield's geometry. On edits preserve its bone and the hand's authored pose.
